How the rig decides

When does a column get watered?

ESP1 looks at a column only during its watering times, and starts a run when that column's soil is drier than Soil start % (35 % unless changed on the System tab). It waters a column at most once in each watering time. The run delivers the column's set amount of water, then stops.

Default windows, one a day: column A 06:00–08:00, column B 08:00–10:00, column C 06:00–08:00. On Zone profiles you can give a column its own times instead, up to 4 a day. Several a day needs ESP1's firmware from 5 Nov 2026; older firmware has one a day.

When are nutrients added?

Only for a column set to Water + nutrients. When it is due for water, ESP1 compares the soil probe's N, P and K with that column's targets. If any one is short by at least the Fertigation gap (30 mg/kg unless changed on the System tab), it mixes nutrients into the water; otherwise it waters only.

It also waters only when the probe isn't answering, or reads under 15 % moisture, because dry soil gives the probe false nutrient numbers. After nutrients, a plain-water flush (20 % of the water) rinses the line.

Nutrients go in at most once a day: once a column has had them, from a scheduled run or a forced one, its other waterings that day are plain water. The column's card says so. (ESP1's firmware from 5 Nov 2026.)

Why didn't a column water today?

Any one of these stops a scheduled run: the column is off; it is outside its watering times; it was already watered in this one (on the rig default, today); the soil isn't drier than Soil start %; the reservoir is under 15 %; actuations are disabled; a fault is held; someone is using Manual/Test or the LCD menu; the mixing tank needs checking; ESP1 has had no recent soil readings; or the column is resting for 30 minutes after repeated faults.

The Diagnostics tab's recent events and ESP1's SD log say which one it was.

Does the rig correct pH?

No. pH is checked, not corrected: after mixing, ESP1 checks the batch's pH and EC and reports PH_FAIL or EC_FAIL if they are outside the safe range, but nothing adds pH adjuster by itself. The pH up and down pumps only run from Manual/Test.

What does a forced run skip?

A forced run ignores the watering times, the soil reading and the once-in-each-time limit, though one that runs inside a column's watering time counts as that time's watering. It never skips safety: ESP1 still refuses it if actuations are disabled, a fault is held, the reservoir is too low, another run or test is going, or the mixing tank needs checking.

Why is there no button for a single valve?

A valve only opens as one step of a full run that ESP1 and ESP2 sequence together, and closes again at the end of it. A button for one valve could leave it open with nothing to confirm it closed. To move water, use a forced run on Controls, or the booster tests on Manual/Test, which open the column's valve only while they run.

How to

Set a column's watering time
  1. Open Zone profiles and find the column's card.
  2. Press Edit settings.
  3. Under When it runs, choose My own time. Pick how many Waterings a day (up to 4), then the start and end of each.
  4. Check the You changed line, then press Save to rig.

Each time must start before it ends, on the same day (the rig can't water past midnight), and end before the next one starts. The column is watered at most once in each, and only when its soil is drier than Soil start %. Nutrients go in at most once a day, so later waterings that day are plain water. Times follow the rig's own clock; operators can check and set it on the System tab. To go back to the default, choose Rig default and save.

Several times a day needs ESP1's firmware from 5 Nov 2026. With older firmware the card offers one time.

Rename a column

Press Rename beside the column's name on its card, type the new name (up to 40 characters) and press Save name. Everyone who signs in sees the new name. It is kept on this website only: the LCD and SMS keep ESP1's own name for the column.

Turn a column on or off

Use the switch at the top of the column's card, and confirm. Off means ESP1 stops watering that column, and stops sending its readings to this page. Its settings are kept for when you turn it back on.

Only turn on a column that is wired and plumbed. ESP2 has its own list of columns built into its firmware; if it doesn't include the column, ESP1 reports COL_MISMATCH and skips it for the day.

Change a column's nutrient targets
  1. On the column's card, pick the crop and growth stage under Compared with. This only changes the reference numbers on this page.
  2. Press Edit settings, then Fill from crop profile to copy those numbers, or type your own.
  3. Press Save to rig. Only now does ESP1 use them.

N, P and K are in ppm (the same as mg/kg), from 0 to 2000. pH is from 3 to 9, and is a record only: see Does the rig correct pH?

Add or change a crop profile
  1. Open Zone profiles and scroll to Crop profiles, below the columns.
  2. Press Add a crop for a new plant, or Edit on a crop to change it. Give it a name and, if you like, a variety or seedling type and notes.
  3. List its growth stages in order (up to 10), each with the N, P and K (ppm), pH, EC (mS/cm) and soil moisture (%) it wants, and how many days it lasts if you know.
  4. Press Save crop. It is in every column's crop list straight away.

Operators, and accounts with 1-hour access or Sub-operator, can change crop profiles; everyone else can see them. A crop, or a stage, that a column uses can't be removed until that column picks another. The built-in crops can be changed or removed too, and put back as they were with Restore original, or Restore under Removed built-in crops.

Crop profiles stay on this website. ESP1 only gets their numbers when you copy them into a column with Fill from crop profile and press Save to rig.

Save a column setup to reuse later

In Edit settings, fill in the column the way you want it, type a name under Save these as a setup, and press Save setup. Next time, pick it from Load a saved setup: it fills the form, and nothing reaches the rig until you press Save to rig. Setups are stored on this website only.

Water a column now (forced run)
  1. Open Controls and find Water a column now.
  2. Choose the column and the litres (up to 20). Leave the nutrients at 0 for plain water; any nutrient needs at least 6.3 L.
  3. Keep a countdown (30 seconds is the default), then press Start forced run.
  4. During the countdown, Cancel forced run stops it. Once it has started, only Emergency stop does.
Stop everything now

Press Emergency stop, at the top of every tab. ESP1 switches every pump and valve off and cuts ESP2's power. Wait for ESP1's reply: if ESP1 is offline nothing has stopped yet, so use the physical cutoff, press MODE + BACK on the unit, or text STOP,ALL.

When it is safe again, press Return to normal in the banner. ESP1 powers ESP2 up and checks it before anything runs.

Stop the rig running anything during maintenance

On Controls, switch Pumps and valves off and confirm. Nothing runs (schedule, forced runs, exercises, tests) until someone switches it back on, even after a restart. Readings, logging and this website carry on. The banner at the top of the page offers Switch pumps and valves back on too, and at the rig it is Settings, Enable Actuations.

Switching them off works even while the rig is locked, because it can only stop things.

Lock or unlock the rig

The rig lock locks this website's controls and the buttons on the rig's screen together. Use it while nobody is looking after the rig, or so that a visitor can't change anything. Automatic watering carries on as normal.

  1. Use the Rig lock switch at the top of the page, or on the System tab, and confirm.
  2. It shows Locking… until ESP1's report confirms it, usually within a few seconds.
  3. To unlock, use the same switch, or Unlock the rig in the banner.

Operators, and accounts with 1-hour access or Sub-operator, can lock and unlock. While it is locked, anyone with control access can still press Emergency stop, switch pumps and valves off, and cancel a forced run. Nothing else can be sent until it is unlocked.

At the rig, MODE + BACK still stops everything, BACK still cancels a forced run's countdown, and holding UP and DOWN together unlocks it, which unlocks this website too. Locking at the rig (Settings, Lock Screen) locks this website as well. ESP1 won't lock from the website while someone at the rig is in its menus, Testing or Calibration.

Deal with a held fault

A held fault (for example FLOW_FAIL, no water moving) pauses the run with every pump off. ESP2 stays powered and remembers how much water is in the mixing tank. The red banner asks you to choose:

  • Hold (wait): stay paused while you look into it.
  • Release tank: send the mixing tank's water to its column as it is, then end the run.
  • Only irrigate: top up with plain water and deliver it, without nutrients.
  • Resume normal: carry on with the run where it stopped.

Ask me again in 2 minutes only dims the banner for now; the fault stays held until someone chooses. While the rig is locked these choices are off, so unlock it first.

If a no-flow fault keeps coming back, the banner suggests Release tank, because resuming would only stop again. On the 4th hold of the same fault in a row, ESP1 cancels the run itself and rests that column for 30 minutes.

Test a pump or valve

Open Manual/Test (operators, and accounts an operator has given access) and press Proceed. The rig leaves automatic while you are there.

  • Timed test: runs one pump or valve for 1–15 seconds and reports whether water moved.
  • Hold-to-run test: runs only while you hold the button, for up to 15 seconds. It starts 3–6 seconds after you press, and stops within 4 seconds of letting go.
  • Pump exercise: the rig's own short maintenance run of one pump.
  • Flow meter check: reads all eight flow meters, to compare with a test.

Leaving the tab hands the rig back to automatic. Nothing here works while the rig is locked.

Approve a new account, and give it more

Open User Management. New accounts are listed under Waiting for approval; press Approve. An approved account can only look at the Live dashboard, Zone profiles and Help until you give it more:

  • Give zone editing: it can change the column cards.
  • Give control: it can use the Controls tab, Diagnostics and the Emergency stop.
  • Give 1-hour access or Make Sub-operator: everything above, plus Manual/Test, System, the rig lock and crop profiles.

Each one has a matching button to take it back. Taking back zone editing or control ends only that access: the account can still look, and can ask again.

Answer access requests

Accounts ask for what they need: a new account for approval, a restricted one to be unrestricted, and an approved one for zone editing or control. A request pops up as soon as it arrives while you are signed in, and the User Management tab shows how many are waiting.

They are listed at the top of User Management. Approve, Unrestrict, Give zone editing or Give control accepts one; Decline tells the account, which can ask again. Blocked accounts can't ask, and Sub-operators are only ever made by an operator.

Ask for more access

An approved account starts by only looking: the Live dashboard, Zone profiles and Help. To change the columns, press Request to edit zone profiles at the top of Zone profiles. To use the controls and see Diagnostics, press Request control on the Controls tab.

An operator sees your request straight away and answers it, and the page changes by itself when they do. If they decline, you can ask again. You can take a request back while it is waiting.

Set the rig's clock
  1. Open System and find Clock. Now on ESP1 is the time the rig is using.
  2. Press Fill in the time now, or type a date and time.
  3. Press Set ESP1's clock and confirm.

Every watering time follows this clock, so check it after a power cut or if a column waters at the wrong time. If Clock chip says Not OK, the clock hardware needs looking at.

Find out why something isn't working

Open Diagnostics. The Summary lists anything ESP1 reports as wrong, in words, or says that everything it checks looks fine. The details under it show each item ESP1 reports, and Recent events on the rig lists faults, refusals and run results.

If ESP1 isn't reporting at all, the page says so and explains what the LCD's GSM + WiFi page shows.

Words used on this site

Column (zone)

One growing column with its own soil probes and its own valve: A, B or C. Column C is off on this rig unless it has been wired and turned on.

ESP1, ESP2 and the Nano

ESP1 is the main controller: it decides, keeps the schedule, logs, sends texts and talks to this website. ESP2 runs the pumps and valves and measures the water; ESP1 powers it up only when something needs to run. The Nano reads the soil, air, light and tank sensors. Restart a board on Controls restarts one of them; the LCD calls this Reboot.

Reservoir and mixing tank

The reservoir holds the plain water. The transfer pump moves water from it into the mixing tank, where nutrients are added and stirred by the mixer. The booster pump then sends the batch to a column.

Irrigation and fertigation

Irrigation is plain water. Fertigation is water with nutrients mixed in, followed by a plain-water flush.

Nutrients A, B and C

The three nutrient pumps, one stock solution each: A is nitrogen (N), B is phosphorus (P), C is potassium (K). A fourth pump (D) is wired but not used.

N, P, K, ppm and targets

Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium in the soil, in ppm (parts per million, the same as mg/kg), as read by the column's 7-in-1 probe. A column's targets are the levels ESP1 aims for; it adds nutrients when a reading falls short by the Fertigation gap.

Soil moisture and NPK probe moisture

Soil moisture is the column's main reading, from its two moisture probes, blended with the NPK probe's own moisture when the two agree. ESP1 compares it with Soil start % to decide when to water. NPK probe moisture is the NPK probe's reading on its own.

Soil EC and soil pH

Soil EC is how many dissolved nutrients the soil holds, in µS/cm: higher means richer. Soil pH is how acidic (under 7) or alkaline (over 7) the soil is. Both come from the 7-in-1 probe.

Mix tank pH and EC

The mixed batch's pH and EC (in mS/cm), measured by ESP2 during a run. Between runs they show the last batch.

Air temperature, humidity and light

Measured at the rig by the Nano, for monitoring only: none of them starts or stops a run. Light is in lux.

Main battery, solar and second battery

The main battery runs the rig; its current says charging or discharging when ESP1 knows the direction, and its charge is an estimate. Solar shows what the panel gives now, and the energy it has given since the daily reset; 0 V at night is normal. The second battery is meant to run the controllers while the inverter runs. Boards run from says which battery they use now: always the main one while battery switching is off in the firmware. If battery switching resets the controllers twice within 24 hours, ESP1 latches a battery switch fault that blocks the inverter until it is cleared on the System tab.

Soil start %

A column is only watered when its soil moisture is below this. Set on the System tab, under Watering thresholds (35 % unless changed). 0 % would mean no column is ever dry enough, so scheduled watering would stop.

Fertigation gap

How far below its target (in mg/kg) a nutrient must be before ESP1 adds nutrients. Set on the System tab, under Watering thresholds (30 unless changed). Smaller means nutrients are added more often.

Watering times (rig default or my own time)

The times of day a column may be watered. Rig default uses the one window built into the firmware (A 06:00–08:00, B 08:00–10:00, C 06:00–08:00). My own time uses the times you set, up to 4 a day with ESP1's firmware from 5 Nov 2026. Either way the column is watered at most once in each, and only when its soil is dry enough. Under the times, the card says how many of today's are done.

What it does (water + nutrients, or water only)

Water + nutrients lets ESP1 add nutrients when the soil needs them. Water only never adds nutrients to that column. On the LCD these are called Auto and Irrigation only.

Crop profile

The crop and growth stage you pick for a column on this website. It sets the reference numbers the soil readings are compared with, and what Fill from crop profile copies. The crops, their stages and their numbers are listed under Crop profiles, below the columns, where they can be changed: see Add or change a crop profile. ESP1 never sees it.

Forced run and countdown

A run you start yourself from Controls, with your own litres and nutrients. It waits for a countdown first (0–300 seconds) so you can still cancel it, and shows ESP1's own countdown on the page.

Emergency stop

Stops every pump and valve at once and cuts ESP2's power. The rig stays stopped until someone presses Return to normal, here or at the LCD.

Actuations disabled (pumps and valves off)

A standing stop: nothing moves, while sensors, logging and this website keep working. Controls' Pumps and valves switch sets it, and ESP1 shows its state as SAFE_MODE. It survives a restart, and lasts until someone switches pumps and valves back on. It is a different thing from the rig lock, which only locks the controls.

Held fault

A serious problem during a run (no flow, power trouble, a dose that took too long) that pauses the run instead of ending it, so the water already in the mixing tank isn't lost. ESP1 waits for you to choose how to continue: see Deal with a held fault.

Manual mode

While the Manual/Test tab is open, ESP1 keeps the rig out of automatic, so no scheduled run can start under your hands. It lasts while the page keeps asking, and someone at the LCD can take it back at any time.

Timed test and hold-to-run test

Two ways to switch one pump or valve on from Manual/Test: a timed test runs for the seconds you set and then reports the flow it counted; a hold-to-run test runs only while you keep the button pressed. ESP1 calls the timed test a pulse.

Pump exercise

The rig runs each AC pump for a few seconds every 2 days, so it doesn't seize between runs. Turn it off if the tank is empty.

ESP2 sweep (reading ESP2's sensors)

Powers ESP2 up for 10–120 seconds to read its own sensors (pH, EC, current, flow meters), then powers it down. Only when the rig is idle. Read ESP2's sensors on Diagnostics and Read the flow meters on Manual/Test both start one.

Readings before conversion

The sensors' own numbers, before ESP1 turns them into percentages and units: soil probe counts, tank distances in cm, and the NPK probe's registers. A sensor stuck at its limit shows here even when the converted reading looks normal. A reading older than 5 minutes is marked STALE.

Recent events on the rig

The last 12 notable things ESP1 logged: faults, runs starting and their results, tests from the website or the LCD, refused requests and SMS traffic. Routine housekeeping is left out. The full history is in ESP1's daily log on its SD card; this list starts again when ESP1 restarts.

Snapshot (live data)

ESP1 sends this website a full report every minute, or every 20 seconds during a run. If none arrives for about 2 minutes, the page says ESP1 isn't reporting and turns off the controls that need it.

Restore factory settings (Restore Defaults)

Puts every column's mode, targets, watering times and ESP1's own name for it, and the thresholds, back to the firmware's starting values. It keeps calibration, which columns are on, and the WiFi setup, and the website's own names and crop profiles aren't touched. Only when the rig is idle; it can't be undone.

Accounts and what each can do

Pending (new), rejected and restricted accounts see only a page with a button to ask for approval or to be unrestricted. Blocked accounts can't use the site at all. Approved accounts can look at the Live dashboard, Zone profiles and Help. With zone editing they can change the column cards, and with control they can use the Controls tab, Diagnostics and the Emergency stop; each is asked for and given separately. Sub-operators and 1-hour access have all of that, plus Manual/Test, System, the rig lock and crop profiles. Operators manage the accounts.

1-hour access and Sub-operator

Ways for an operator to open Manual/Test and System, and the rig lock, to an approved account: 1-hour access ends by itself after an hour, and Sub-operator lasts until an operator removes it. Restricting, blocking or rejecting the account ends both at once.

States and replies

ESP1 states
  • IDLE_STATE: waiting; ready to run.
  • ACTIVE_STATE: a run or pump exercise is in progress.
  • TEST_MODE: a timed pulse or hold-to-run test is running.
  • SAFE_MODE: idle with actuations disabled.
  • EMERGENCY_STOP: stopped, by an emergency stop or a held fault.
  • STARTUP_SYNC: checking ESP2 after a restart or a stop.
  • RECOVERY_STATE: ESP2 stopped answering and ESP1 is bringing it back.
Command replies
  • queued: sent, waiting for ESP1 to read it. ESP1 reads one every 3 seconds.
  • received: ESP1 has it and is checking it.
  • accepted: ESP1 started it, or armed its countdown.
  • completed: done.
  • rejected: ESP1 refused it, and says why.
  • failed: it started but didn't finish, and ESP1 says why.
  • withdrawn: you took it back before ESP1 read it.
Current run (its phases)

starting: ESP2 is powering up. running: water is being moved, in steps (fill, dose, mix, release, flush). finishing: the run is over and the LCD shows its result for up to 20 seconds. error: the LCD is listing problems from the run.
